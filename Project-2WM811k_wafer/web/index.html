Phase 0: Wafer-Map and Process Context Setup

Goal: Understand the semiconductor meaning of wafer maps before treating them as standard images.

The Diagnostic Value of Wafer Maps

A wafer map directly answers a fundamental manufacturing question: Is a specific equipment malfunction or process drift causing our semiconductor yields to drop?

By mapping the spatial coordinates of failed chips, engineers trace failures back to the physical footprint of the machines that processed them. Because every tool interacts with the wafer geometrically, the spatial pattern isolates the root cause from thousands of potential variables.

Why This Isn't Generic Image Classification

  • Spatial Reality: Pixels are not abstract light values; they represent exact physical coordinates of discrete physical objects (dies).
  • Noise Context: In standard images, noise is an artifact. In wafer maps, random noise (isolated failed dies) is normal baseline yield loss. We are looking specifically for structured noise (systematic patterns).
  • Sparsity and Imbalance: Most dies pass, and extreme defect patterns are rare anomalies. A model must differentiate between a perfectly good wafer with random normal defects and one with a subtle systematic issue.
